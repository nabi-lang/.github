# Nabi name and logo

The code and docs of Nabi are open source. The name "Nabi" and the Nabi
logo are not covered by those licenses; they tell people what is the
official Nabi. This policy says how you may use them. It is meant to be
permissive: if your use is honest about what it is, it is almost certainly
fine.

The logo files are in [nabi-lang/nabi `assets/`](https://github.com/nabi-lang/nabi/tree/main/assets)
(`logo.svg`, `wordmark/`, `icons/`).

## Fine without asking

- Referring to Nabi: in articles, talks, books, courses, videos, social
  media, a list of supported languages, a comparison.
- Saying what your project does with Nabi: "written in Nabi", "a Nabi
  library for X", "Nabi support for editor Y", "compatible with Nabi 0.1".
- Using the logo next to such a statement, or to link to nabi-lang.org,
  unchanged except for size, and with enough space around it.
- Naming a package `nabi-something` or `something-nabi`, or a community
  group "Nabi users of <city>", as long as the next point holds.
- Distributing unmodified official release archives, or packaging them
  for an OS distribution with only the changes packaging needs (paths,
  build flags, a separate package for each tool).

## Not without permission

- Implying that your project, product, event or service is official,
  endorsed or maintained by the Nabi project when it is not. Make it clear
  who runs it.
- Calling a modified build "Nabi" or "official Nabi". A fork or a build
  with changes beyond packaging may say it is "based on Nabi" but must use
  a different name and must not use the logo as its own.
- Using the name or logo as, or as part of, your own product name,
  company name, logo or domain in a way that could confuse people about
  who made it.
- Changing the logo (other than size) or combining it with another mark.

If you are unsure, or want to do something listed under "not without
permission", ask in
[GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/nabi-lang/nabi/discussions). We
say yes to most requests.

The Nabi name and logo are held by DaminStudio and looked after on behalf of
the Nabi project (nabi-lang). If the project moves to a neutral foundation,
they move with it. This policy does not limit what the open source licenses
allow you to do with the code itself.
